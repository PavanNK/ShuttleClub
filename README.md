# Shuttle Club for Apple Vision Pro

A native SwiftUI / RealityKit badminton prototype based on the supplied colourful indoor-club image. All gameplay geometry, textures, character construction and sound cues are authored locally in code. No external asset-generation service was used for this build.

**Status:** signed Release **1.0 (11)**, `com.shuttleclub.vision`, was installed on the user's Vision Pro on **12 October 2026**, and an independent app query confirms that version. Remote launch timed out after 30 seconds; a separate process query found no running app, so **open Shuttle Club manually**. Build 11 passes **209 native tests in 22 suites** in **28.430 seconds**. It adds delayed natural-hold Sense alignment, shaft-preserving face turns, virtual court movement, floating Milo and genuine-contact visual feedback. [Build 11 evidence](Verification/Build11/README.md) records software checks and reviewed final-source simulator captures using artificial input; the [installation summary](Verification/Build11/Device-Install-Summary.json) records the subsequent user-authorised deployment. Physical grip, gameplay, controller fit, movement comfort, latency, pairing/haptics, headset frame rate and audio remain unverified. Shared physics is unchanged; its last direct run passed 16 tests in build 9.

## Play on Vision Pro

Requires visionOS 26 or later and Xcode 27 for this source project.

1. Open **Shuttle Club** manually from the Vision Pro launcher; installed version 1.0 (11) is independently confirmed. To build from source, open `ShuttleClub.xcodeproj`, choose the ShuttleClub scheme and your connected Vision Pro, select your own development team for signing, then Run.
2. The default **Minimal** home follows approved option B, with a wordmark above the court, one **Play with Milo** button and compact Racket, game-mode and gear controls. The gear opens simple home settings: choose **Playful** for approved option A, or open tracking / racket-hand / pace setup and sound / comfort. Your home style is saved locally. The game-mode control shows the selected Free play, Quick match or Target rush mode.
3. Open **Racket** for the wide approved option C chooser. Three cards show actual 3D rackets against a dimmed colour background; the world behind it is not blurred. Tap a card to preview it, then **Choose** to save; Back cancels an unconfirmed preview. Coral and Mint remain visible, and **Also try Solar / Midnight** switches the third card. All four virtual presets remain available. The home displays the saved racket in the foreground without a pedestal; changes are allowed between rallies.
4. Enter the court and allow the requested tracking permission. Stay in place and use small, controlled swings.
5. For hands, **open your selected hand, then close it** to grab. The faint preview becomes solid when held. The racket locks to the wrist; relaxed fingers or temporarily missing fingertip data do not release it. **Open fully for about 0.3 seconds** to release; at least three visible fingertips must agree that the hand is open. Partial finger visibility and menu pinching keep the grip held. Release or wrist-tracking loss pauses an active rally; grab/recover, then choose Resume. **Align angle** fits an upright racket to a comfortable ready pose when idle or paused, and saves the angle separately for each hand; **Fine-tune angle** adjusts front/back tilt, side tilt and face turn without moving the handle; changes save for that hand. **Reset hand fit** restores that hand’s default angle. A slow close retains the measured palm mount while the wrist stays tracked. Missing fingertips can use measured finger-base bends for acquisition, while release still requires observed fingertips. Backgrounding interrupts unfinished gestures while preserving an acquired wrist mount, and readiness requires a fresh foreground sample. If the initial palm/knuckles are missing, setup asks you to show them before grabbing. For Sense, open the court menu → **Racket & tracking** → **Fit your racket**. Use the free hand to choose **Align from my hold**, then hold the racket controller naturally and still for the three-second countdown. Movement restarts the stable-hold window; tracking loss, leaving setup, backgrounding or input/hand changes cancel capture. A new default controller fit is aligned before its first automatic serve. Existing chosen fits are preserved. In **Angle**, front/back and side tilt adjust the shaft, while **−90° / Centre / +90°** turns the face around that shaft. Position follows the controller grip. Fits save separately for each controller; **Reset fit** restores the selected hand. **Recenter** aligns the court to your facing direction. Input mode and racket-hand selection survive relaunch.
6. Open **Move in court** in setup or the court menu. **Thumbstick slide** uses the free Sense controller's stick, or the sole connected controller's stick when only one is available. **Auto glide** moves the virtual court toward a receiving position; **Stay centred** keeps it fixed and aims feeds at your current spot. Hands use Auto glide when the saved selection is Thumbstick slide, and can choose Stay centred. These modes support playing from one comfortable physical spot; physical walking is not required. You still make every racket hit.
7. Return from tracking setup with **Back to court**, which closes nested court panels. Keep the selected racket held and tracked for two ready seconds after any controller alignment finishes. Milo serves automatically, with a visible 0.65-second windup, and feeds the next rally after each point. Opening a panel, pausing, losing tracking or entering the background resets readiness; a completed match stops automatic serves. For an earlier serve, open the free hand and hold a deliberate pinch, or press the Sense trigger. A pending alignment blocks serves. Return the shuttle by swinging the tracked racket into it.
8. Tracking loss pauses an active rally. Bring the hand/controller into view and choose Resume after tracking recovers. Pause, Restart and Leave remain session controls.

For PS VR2 Sense, pair the left and right controllers individually in Vision Pro's Bluetooth settings before playing. The chosen racket hand selects the corresponding controller. Trigger requests an earlier serve; Menu toggles pause when no court panel is open. It cannot resume a rally behind an open panel. Leaving the court clears pause after stopping tracking and flight, restoring the configured lobby music level. Haptics are generated on racket contact when the controller exposes a haptic engine. Ordinary non-spatial gamepads are not treated as tracked rackets.

Apple's implementation guidance: [Explore spatial accessory input on visionOS](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/289/).

## Rackets and play

The racket menu uses the actual locally constructed 3D models, including string beds and wrapped grips. Each card has a gently turning preview and a short handling description; reduced motion keeps the preview still. Browsing does not change the saved racket until Choose is pressed. Effective collision mass and rebound coefficients vary between virtual presets; the Midnight model also has a smaller string bed. These presets do not change the physical weight of a Sense controller.

Tracked returns use measured racket contact, contact-point velocity, face normal and distance from the string-bed centre. A world-space sweep interpolates the racket pose and verifies the cork-to-string distance at contact, avoiding false contacts created by rotating local endpoint chords. Measured angular motion contributes at the actual string point, including turns around a stationary string centre. Impact-point speeds of 45 m/s or more are rejected as unreliable motion. Hand-grip capture preserves the open-palm mount relative to a measured wrist. Native hand head velocity uses acquisition timestamps rather than the render interval; repeated anchors add no new swing speed. Preview rackets cannot hit or start a serve. Duplicate or reordered native packets cannot replace a held world pose or add velocity, and tracking loss immediately disarms contact history. Virtual court translation remaps cached controller poses into the current court frame; impact energy comes from measured world motion, so navigation cannot create swing energy. Controller angular motion contributes to head velocity. A moving, finite-mass string-bed collision sets the returned shuttle velocity; tracked player shots receive no automatic landing correction. The visible controller racket and collision both use the same measured pose. Sense grip and anchor conversion use ARKit's `.rendered` correction to align with the physical controller in passthrough. Saved per-hand position and quaternion / angle adjustments are applied before both pose conversion and angular lever-arm velocity. Face turn now remains shaft-local after tilting; legacy saved fits migrate without changing their mounted pose. A fit change discards swing history and restarts readiness; its first fresh sample is a contact-disabled baseline. Stale, low-accuracy or implausible tracking is rejected. Invalid controller angular motion cannot become a stationary pose. Controller device/accessory mappings publish together after refresh, and polling is disabled during that refresh. One accessory restart is allowed for a stopped provider; repeated failure or a stopped shared world provider requires Retry tracking with a new session. A brief flash marks genuine string contact, and the feather skirt turns toward the changed airflow after impact; these effects do not change the cork trajectory or add aerodynamic spin forces.

The shuttle uses gravity and quadratic air drag with bounded substeps. The drag coefficient, racket mass transfer, restitution, grip orientation and string-bed tolerances are provisional tuning estimates, not measured matches to a real racket or shuttle. A still image does not contain the motion data needed to establish such a match. There is no spin-resolved feather aerodynamics model.

Milo starts serves at his visible strings with a numerically planned launch. Subsequent returns require swept cork-to-string contact with his actual visible racket and use the same moving-racket impact model as player returns. His planner selects a contact that his arm can reach and power within its 18 rad/s limit, then aims toward the player's hitting area. It includes the cork radius, off-centre restitution and tangential transfer before the stroke; it does not correct the outgoing velocity after impact. He approaches at bounded speeds, adjusts his racket angle to retain shoulder leverage, follows through and recovers to his ready position. Milo now floats without legs; his head watches the shuttle and his body glides during approach and recovery. His string face stays aimed in world space while the shoulder moves. Robot stroke and shuttle contact advance together in intervals no longer than 1/240 second, avoiding a whole-frame head-motion average at impact. Tests exercise weak shots and high lobs at 30 / 60 / 90 Hz. Unreachable shots miss. Speed, reaction and positioning error vary across Friendly, Club and Challenge. Quick match is first to seven, win by two. Target rush scores landing targets and streaks. Stationary play and quick matches are arcade adaptations rather than complete tournament badminton rules.

Court, lobby and racket-card frame subscriptions coalesce queued callbacks into one pending update while preserving their full elapsed time. Scene teardown cancels queued work; a long court interruption retains the existing pause policy. Reattachment replaces old lobby previews, reuses shuttle geometry and installs each racket-card studio light once. A 256-entry LRU shares exact mesh resources across repeated geometry without changing materials, tessellation, entity transforms or collision parents. Evicting a cached reference does not invalidate live entities. These changes reduce construction and queued work; headset frame rate and thermal behaviour remain unmeasured.

## Original scene

The immersive scene includes a turquoise court, white court lines, net and purple posts, procedural maple floor, coral / yellow / violet mural, tall windows, garden foliage, benches, plants, bag, ceiling beams and lights, a feathered shuttle, and an orange / ivory Milo robot with cyan face. A rear wall, closed entry and lockers enclose the room behind the player; continuous procedural daylight covers the outdoor view beyond the windows. Build 6 uses calmer court grain, removes baked architectural shadow bands from that texture, and uses a 17,000-intensity directional key with a 5,000-intensity soft fill. Scene lighting supplies the architectural shadows.

`App/ClubScene.swift` builds the scene. `Resources/VisualReference.png` stores the supplied art reference. The court and racket previews are rendered 3D geometry; the approved menu compositions are implemented in SwiftUI with locally drawn glyphs. Reference and mockup PNGs are not used as scene backdrops.

Build 8 tracking setup captures are [home setup](Preview/Build8-Home-Tracking.png) and [court setup](Preview/Build8-Court-Tracking.png). They are actual native simulator pixels; hands and controllers are unavailable in that simulator. The setup has contrasting selected input/hand controls, one shared status card, angle alignment and reset, with pace/retry/recenter inside a disclosure.

Approved home/menu captures are [Minimal home](Preview/ApprovedMenus/Home-Minimal.png), [Playful home](Preview/ApprovedMenus/Home-Playful.png), [racket selection](Preview/ApprovedMenus/Racket-Selection.png) and [home settings](Preview/ApprovedMenus/Home-Settings.png). Native visual inspection covered composition, text legibility, control sizing and the actual 3D racket models. The Minimal and Playful captures were refreshed from the final sources after the passing test run. [App icon](Preview/ApprovedMenus/App-Icon.png) previews the locally drawn racket / shuttle design. `Tools/make-app-icon.js` reproduces its three 1024×1024 layers in `Resources/Assets.xcassets/AppIcon.solidimagestack`; visionOS applies the circular mask and layer depth. Asset inspection confirmed all three vision / 2x layers and a SolidImageStack in the signed `Assets.car`, plus AppIcon metadata in `CFBundleIcons`. Launcher appearance still requires device verification.

To regenerate the existing original icon layers, install the optional local art-tool dependency and Chromium: run `npm install`, `npx playwright install chromium`, and `npm run app-icon` from `Tools/`. The game itself uses the committed icon PNGs and does not require Node.js or Playwright.

`Preview/SimpleMenu-Build4.png` is the historical build 4 / 5 menu, superseded by the approved build 6 layouts. The older `Preview/RacketMenu.png`, `Preview/SimulatorCourt.png` and `Preview/RacketContact.png` are also actual simulator captures. `Preview/RacketContact.mp4` records the running native simulator with artificial hand poses; it shows incoming flight and then freezes at contact. The contact capture pauses at the collision time with the cork touching the strings; its debug inspection scene is offset 0.8 m away from the simulator camera to keep the impact visible. This changes the inspection framing, not the relative contact positions.
